RESPOND Safeguarding Framework

Scenario Library

Reference scenarios and response scripts for staff in schools and other settings working with children and young people. Search, filter, and open the full guides.

How to Use These Scenarios

Each scenario shows how the seven RESPOND steps apply to a real-world situation. These scenarios are reference material, not a training course. Use them to check how to respond or to prepare for a conversation. They do not replace your school's safeguarding training, policy or the Designated Safeguarding Lead's (DSL's) advice on a specific case.

Worried About a Child Now?

Don't wait until you've finished reading. Tell your DSL or a deputy as soon as is practicably possible.

If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.

Some scenarios describe abuse and harm. Take a break whenever you need to. Education Support offers free, confidential help for education staff, 24/7: 08000 562 561.

4Neglect: Sudden Withdrawal / Possible Neglect
Neglect

Over the past month, a previously engaged Year 6 student has become increasingly withdrawn, frequently arrives hungry without lunch money, wears the same unwashed clothes.

Quick notes (earlier edition)

RecogniseHunger, fatigue, poor hygiene, inappropriate clothing, repeated lateness.
EngageGentle curiosity; avoid assumptions about families.
SupportSafety first: are they safe right now? Offer a safe, quiet space and reassurance. Your calm is contagious.
Pause90-second grounding, then the Three Lenses: Risk, Rights, Resources. Don't investigate, blame or promise secrecy.
OfferModel alternatives: explain the support available (school nurse, counsellor, trusted adult) and what will happen next. If in doubt, N before O.
NotifyTell the DSL as soon as is practicably possible; the DSL coordinates next steps with other agencies. If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.
DocumentGOLD: Ground, Observe, Language, Do. Record the child's exact words, as soon as is practicably possible.
